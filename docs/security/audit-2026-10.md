# Security Audit — October 2026

**Repository:** `mobile` (Railroad Network mobile client)
**Commit audited:** `23db15763e7bf858dd5ff8358810cf14cdf99110` (2026-09-22)
**Performed by:** Claude (Anthropic AI model, Fable 5.1), operated by the project maintainer
**Date:** 2026-10-02
**Companion report:** the `station` repository's `docs/security/audit-2026-10.md`
(findings `RRN-A-013` onward), which covers the Rust core the app calls through

## Disclosure and limitations

This audit was performed by an AI model, not by a professional security firm. It
is an internal review intended to raise the floor, not an attestation:

- It is a **code review**, not a penetration test. Nothing was run on a device
  or simulator; library behaviour (`react-native-keychain`, `@react-navigation`,
  React Native's `fetch`) was established by reading the installed sources under
  `node_modules`, not by executing them. Two reviewer instances read the app in
  full (custody and recovery; network and screens) and every finding was then
  re-checked against the source by the lead reviewer. One finding's station-side
  consequence was reproduced by a test in the station repository and is marked
  *Reproduced*; the rest are *Traced* (confirmed by reading the full path) or
  *Plausible* (mechanism read, one step not confirmed).
- **Absence of a finding is not evidence of absence.**
- A professional audit is still warranted before any deployment where real
  people depend on this software's guarantees.

This report mirrors the station report's format and severity scale. Where a
mobile finding is one half of a station finding it is cross-referenced by its
`RRN-A-###` id.

## Summary

One High, six Medium, twelve Low, one Info. The August assessment of the
custody core holds: the wallet secret never crosses the FFI into JavaScript,
signing and sealing go through the one audited Rust path, every record carries a
domain-separating `kind`, transport nonces are reserved before send, and there
is still a single persistence backend and no deep-link surface. The new
recover-onto-device flow is careful with its inputs and never writes a wrong
key. The findings are about **what the user is shown versus what the phone
signs or does**: amounts rendered without their sign, a payment request dressed
as a payment, a charter re-fetched between reading and signing, a Back button
that re-runs wallet creation with an emptied passphrase — and about the two
short human-compared codes the ceremonies rest on, which August already flagged
and which remain as they were.

## Findings

### RRN-M-011 · High — Amounts on action buttons drop their sign

**Where:** `src/ledger/format.ts:21-27` (`formatCommons` takes `Math.abs`);
`src/screens/main/Inquiry.tsx:593` (`Accept ${formatCommons(acceptCenti)}`),
`:695`, `:316`, `:666-676` (`parseOffer`); `src/screens/main/ListingDetail.tsx:294-304`.
Station half: `RRN-A-015`.

`formatCommons` is documented as formatting an *unsigned* value — the sign is
meant to be rendered separately by `<Amount>`. Every call-to-action that carries
an amount uses it directly. `parseOffer` accepts a negative number ("a Commons
subsidy"), and the station accepts a negative offer, a negative agreed price and
a negative per-period contract amount on any negotiable listing.

A buyer types `-20` as their offer on a provider's negotiable listing. The offer
chip shows `−20.00`; the action button the provider taps says **"Accept 20.00"**;
the banner afterwards says "Both sides agreed at 20.00"; on a recurring listing
the buyer's screen says "You'll be charged 20.00 per week". The signed records
carry −2000. On a recurring Services listing the station's charge sweep then
moves 20.00 from the provider to the buyer every period for the provider's own
stated duration, with no debt-floor or tier check.

**Reproduced** (station side): offer −2000 → agreed −2000 → contract at −2000
per period → after four periods the provider is at −80.00 against a −20.00 floor.

**Recommendation.** Make `formatCommons` sign-preserving or route every
call-to-action through `<Amount signed>`; refuse to render "Accept" for a
negative offer on a non-Commons listing; match `parseOffer` to the sign rule the
station adopts. The station must enforce the rule too (`RRN-A-015`); the app's
job is to never show a number whose sign it has removed next to a verb.

### RRN-M-012 · Medium — A payment request is presented as an incoming payment

**Where:** `src/notifications/eventNotification.ts:28-41`; `src/screens/main/Home.tsx:374-386`;
`src/screens/main/ConfirmReceived.tsx:191-194`, `:232-254`;
`src/screens/main/TransactionDetail.tsx:149`, `:243`; station
`transaction_view.rs:109-110`, `events.rs:172-175`, `engine.rs:242-247`, `:317-324`.

A negative-amount proposal is a payment *request*: by ADR-0018 it debits its
receiver, and the receiver's confirmation is their commitment. The station's
row for the receiver is simply `direction: "in"`, and the phone derives
everything from that. The background notification says "Incoming payment —
20.00 ₡ from … Tap to confirm"; the inbox lists it under "To confirm", captioned
"from {counterparty}"; `ConfirmReceived` says "{counterparty} is paying you" over
a button reading "All good — I received this"; afterwards the detail screen
reads "Received from … / Incoming credit". The only cue is a minus sign and red
colour on the amount. The stock app cannot author such a proposal, but any
custom client can, and the stock app itself emits one after RRN-M-011. The
companion `rrn wallet confirm` has the same gap (`RRN-A-027`).

**Recommendation.** Branch on `amountCenti < 0` in the inbox row, the confirm
screen, the notification and the detail view with distinct copy ("X is asking
you to pay N", Pay / Decline). Until that UI exists, drop negative-amount
proposals from the "To confirm" list.

### RRN-M-013 · Medium — Android Back after wallet creation overwrites the wallet under an empty passphrase

**Where:** `src/screens/onboarding/GenerateWallet.tsx:101-129`;
`src/screens/onboarding/OnboardingContext.tsx:71` (`clearSecrets: () => setPassphrase('')`);
`src/navigation/RootNavigator.tsx:91-100` (`gestureEnabled: false`, iOS-only per
the library's own type docs); `src/screens/onboarding/BiometricSetup.tsx:66-69`;
`src/wallet/Wallet.ts:150-177` (`persistWallet` overwrites unconditionally);
station `rrn-identity/src/wallet.rs:173` (no empty-passphrase check).

After sealing, `GenerateWallet` wipes the context passphrase and replaces itself
with `WalletReady`. The stack beneath is still `[Welcome, Passphrase,
BiometricSetup]`, and the only Back protection is `gestureEnabled: false`, which
the navigation library documents as "Only supported on iOS". The root navigator
stays in onboarding until `WalletReady`'s button adopts the wallet. On Android —
the pilot platform — the system Back button lands on `BiometricSetup`; any choice
there navigates to a fresh `GenerateWallet`, which reads the passphrase from
context (now `''`) and calls `createWallet` (a new keypair) or `persistWallet`
(on the recover path), overwriting the stored wallet with one sealed under the
empty string. Nothing checks passphrase length and nothing refuses an overwrite.

The identity just shown as "yours forever" is gone, and the stored blob has no
passphrase protection. After the first background the user is locked out for
good: the lock screen disables its button on an empty passphrase and offers no
reset. Going Back two screens to `Passphrase` (still populated) and pressing
Continue replaces the identity under the real passphrase, silently.

**Recommendation.** Refuse to run `GenerateWallet` below the minimum passphrase
length; have `persistWallet` fail closed when a wallet exists unless the caller
passes an explicit overwrite flag; reach `WalletReady` by `navigation.reset`;
add `usePreventRemove` on the generation screens; reject an empty passphrase in
the Rust `encrypt` as well (`RRN-A-050`).

### RRN-M-014 · Medium — `SignCharter` signs a body re-fetched at tap time

**Where:** `src/ledger/useLedger.ts:483-507`; `src/screens/main/SignCharter.tsx:108-131`;
`src/wallet/governance.ts:217-259`.

The screen renders the pending charter — community id, principles, rights
floor, founders. On tap, `useSignFoundingCharter` discards it and fetches the
charter again ("sign the freshest body the station holds"), rebuilds the body
from the re-fetched fields, checks that it matches the re-fetched `body_hex`,
and signs. The check is self-consistent for whatever the station chose to
return. A station that serves charter A to the read and charter B to the
sign-time read receives a founder's genuine signature over B. The shipped
station refuses a second ceremony, so this needs a modified or compromised
station; but the on-device read-then-sign is the founder's only check on the
coordinator, and the screen's comment claims "a founder can never sign a body
that differs from the one on offer". Founders are shown truncated
(`rrn1` + 5 … 5) while full keys are signed.

**Recommendation.** Pass the rendered `pending` into the signer and sign exactly
that, or refuse when the re-fetched `body_hex` differs from the displayed one;
show full founder addresses before signing.

### RRN-M-015 · Medium — The phone signs over ids whose meaning comes from station JSON

**Where:** `src/ledger/useLedger.ts:979-1004` (confirm), `:628-651` (verdict),
`:446-470` (vote), `:695-718` (ballot), `:410-436` (co-sign);
`src/wallet/confirmation.ts:70-75`.

A confirmation signs `{kind, proposal_id, confirmer, confirmed_at}`. The
`proposal_id` is the id string from the station's `transactions` reply, and the
amount, counterparty, memo and tier the user approved are other fields of the
same unverified JSON. The phone never receives the signed proposal bytes, so it
cannot recompute the content address it is committing to. The same holds for
juror verdicts and escalation ballots (the juror sees no amount at all) and for
governance votes and co-signs. A hostile station shows "Maria is paying you
+5.00" under the id of a −20.00 payment request and receives a genuine signature
debiting the member.

The threat model accepts that the phone does not re-verify push events; it does
not accept that the phone *signs* over station-described content. Fixing it
needs the station to return canonical signed record bytes and an FFI call that
verifies the signer, recomputes the id and returns the decoded fields.

**Recommendation.** Render and sign from verified record bytes; until then,
document the trust the member places in the station operator at signing time.

### RRN-M-016 · Medium — The pairing code is still 32 bits; a re-announced advertisement replaces a discovered host

**Where:** `src/network/Pairing.ts:43` (`SAS_HEX_LEN = 8`), `:279-314`;
`src/screens/join/Pair.tsx:251-259`; `src/network/Discovery.ts:213`;
station `paired.rs:161-168`, `pairing.rs:117-124`. Station half: `RRN-A-023`.
RRN-M-001 (August) is open and unchanged.

The code is eight hex characters of a hash over the station's and the phone's
long-term public keys — all public inputs, no commitment. Beyond the August
man-in-the-middle argument, two cheaper routes: an attacker on the LAN who
learns a victim's address (it travels in the cleartext `/pair` body) grinds a
key whose code with this station equals the victim's, submits their own `/pair`
during the ceremony, and the operator's confirmation list shows two rows with
the right code — the phone's confirm screen shows only the code, never the
phone's own address, so the operator cannot tell them apart. And both native
discovery modules re-deliver a re-announced instance name, which
`Discovery.ts:213` stores by name, so a spoofed advertisement with the real
instance name replaces the genuine row's host and port with no ARP spoofing.

**Recommendation.** Consume the station's longer, station-bound code when it
lands (`RRN-A-023`); show the phone's short address beside the code; never let a
re-announcement silently replace a discovered row.

### RRN-M-017 · Medium — The recovery fingerprint is 40 bits; VMK ceremonies show a code that cannot match

**Where:** `src/screens/main/HelpRecover.tsx:213-231`;
`src/screens/onboarding/RecoverFromCircle.tsx:354-374`; station
`rrn-identity/src/recovery/ceremony.rs:62-68`, `rrn-station/src/storage/vmk.rs:363-375`.
Station half: `RRN-A-029`, `RRN-A-030`.

The fingerprint that closed RRN-M-002 is ten hex characters of a hash of the
request's ephemeral key, with no commitment. In person, where the holder scans
the owner's screen, it is redundant; its only value is for the relayed
ceremonies the copy invites ("in person or on a call"), and there a 40-bit match
can be manufactured by a key search within the hours a ceremony stays open. The
same request format is used for the station's encrypted-volume unlock, whose
console prints a *different* algorithm; the holder screen's instruction "it
must match their screen exactly" is therefore unsatisfiable for every legitimate
volume unlock, and holders will learn to ignore the mismatch.

**Recommendation.** Consume the unified, longer fingerprint from the station
repository (`RRN-A-029`/`030`) and update the fixture assertion; until then,
narrow the normative copy to in-person scanning.

### RRN-M-018 · Low — Replies are bound by nonce only, and the nonce restarts

**Where:** `src/network/StationClient.ts:981-1026`, `:897-907`;
`src/network/stationNonce.ts:91-102`; station `rpc_envelope.rs:183-194`,
`paired.rs:101-112`. Station half: `RRN-A-024`. Supersedes RRN-M-009.

`openReply` checks the station signature, the version and the echoed nonce.
After a restore from export, a circle recovery, or a factory reset and re-pair,
the phone's counter starts again at one and the station resets its high-water
mark, with the keys unchanged — so every reply recorded before verifies again
for the new request at the same nonce. Write hooks ignore the reply body, so a
recorded success replayed by an on-path party makes the app report a payment or
dispute as submitted when the station never saw it. Fix is station-side (a
request hash in the reply) plus a check here.

### RRN-M-019 · Low — A held shard is silently replaced; the holder address is never checked

**Where:** `src/screens/main/HeldShards.tsx:69-116`; `src/wallet/heldShards.ts:64-71`
(`shards[shard.originalAddress] = shard`); station `flow.rs:223-234`, `:326-356`.

A shard payload is unauthenticated CBOR. Scanning one replaces whatever this
device held for the same owner, without confirmation; `holderAddress` is stored
and never compared to the wallet's own address; the success notice does not
name the owner. Anyone who gets N−K+1 holders to scan a fabricated payload for
Alice ("I'm re-doing my recovery, scan mine") makes Alice unrecoverable, which
she learns when she tries. The threat model calls a planted shard "harmless to
the holder". The FFI comments say the holder app confirms the shard is addressed
to it; it does not.

### RRN-M-020 · Low — The refresh screen says old pieces "stop working"

**Where:** `src/screens/recovery/RecoveryIntro.tsx:63-69`; station `flow.rs:165-180`.

A refresh is a fresh split of the *same* secret; the old shards remain a valid
K-of-N sharing of the key indefinitely. A member who removes three distrusted
holders is told their pieces "become useless"; those three can still rebuild the
key together. The Rust documentation is accurate; the in-app copy says the
opposite. Reword, and point members who distrust K holders at a new identity.

### RRN-M-021 · Low — Android keystore gates are weaker than documented

**Where:** `src/crypto/SecureStore.ts:139-163`; `src/network/backgroundCredential.ts`;
`react-native-keychain` `KeychainModule.kt:79`, `CipherStorageKeystoreAesGcm.kt:160-178`.

The library ignores the `ACCESSIBLE` option on Android and never sets
`setUnlockedDeviceRequired`, so every un-gated entry — including the background
credential and its secret, which together are a full signing wallet — is usable
whenever the process runs, device locked or not. Background sync therefore signs
while the phone is locked, contrary to the code comment and `SECURITY.md`. The
"biometric" gate is created with `AUTH_BIOMETRIC_STRONG | AUTH_DEVICE_CREDENTIAL`
and a five-second validity, so the device PIN satisfies it. These properties hold
on iOS only. Correct the documentation, and for the background credential either
patch key generation or move to the read-scoped token the threat model already
names.

### RRN-M-022 · Low — The biometric choice is never persisted at onboarding

**Where:** `src/screens/onboarding/GenerateWallet.tsx:101-110`; `src/screens/main/Settings.tsx:81`;
`src/wallet/Wallet.ts:262-266`, `:283`.

Only `setBiometricUnlock` writes the profile flag. After "Not now", Settings
shows the gate as on (`p.biometricEnabled ?? true`) over an un-gated entry, and
`changePassphrase` re-saves with `requireBiometric: true` — silently adding a
gate the user declined where a biometric is enrolled, and failing the save
where none is, so a passphrase believed compromised cannot be changed.

### RRN-M-023 · Low — "Lock" drops JavaScript references only

**Where:** `src/wallet/WalletSession.tsx:101`; `src/wallet/Wallet.ts:76-88`;
`src/screens/onboarding/RecoverFromCircle.tsx:183-192`; `src/screens/main/FactoryReset.tsx:36-41`.

The app never calls the generated `uniffiDestroy()`, so the Rust zeroize-on-drop
runs only at garbage collection; every signature clones the secret into a new
handle; the recovery session (ephemeral key plus K shares) stays in component
state after a successful rebuild; factory reset neither locks nor clears the
query cache. Defense in depth against a compromised OS, which is outside the
trust boundary — but the threat model describes the handle as dropped on
background.

### RRN-M-024 · Low — Unauthenticated error bodies shown; no size cap; redirects followed

**Where:** `src/network/StationClient.ts:967-977`, `:1916-1922`;
`src/network/Pairing.ts:252-264`.

On a non-2xx the response body becomes the error message rendered in a danger
banner — unbounded, unauthenticated text from whoever answers at the stored
host ("re-pair with station X"). On 2xx the whole body is buffered before the
seal is opened, bounded only by the timer. No `redirect` option is set.
`Pairing.ts` calls the rejection body "a short, safe reason", which holds for an
honest station only.

### RRN-M-025 · Low — A discovered station's host is not validated and becomes the permanent endpoint

**Where:** `src/network/Discovery.ts:273-275` versus `:329`;
`modules/rrn-discovery/ios/HybridStationDiscovery.swift:316`; `src/network/resolveEndpoint.ts:64-65`.

A manually entered host must match `/^[A-Za-z0-9._-]+$/`; a discovered host is
checked only for non-emptiness, and on iOS the SRV target is passed verbatim
into `http://${host}:${port}/pair`. A relay that forwards pairing honestly
passes the code comparison (the keys are unchanged) and its host is then saved
and used for every later request, since the host hint only ever moves to the
host that just answered. *Plausible*: depends on `dns_sd` leaving URL-significant
characters unescaped in `hosttarget`, which was not exercised.

### RRN-M-026 · Low — The active station is whichever paired address sorts lowest

**Where:** `src/network/useStation.ts:35-38`; `src/network/pairedStation.ts:61`.

`station = data[0]` over a list sorted by address. "Pair with another station"
either silently redirects every read, write and background sync to the new
station or does nothing, depending on how its address sorts, with no indicator.
A rogue operator needs a couple of key generations to sort first.

### RRN-M-027 · Low — Counterparty text is unfiltered and placed where identity or system text is expected

**Where:** `src/screens/main/Home.tsx:338`, `:376`; `src/notifications/eventNotification.ts:37-39`,
`:76-81`; `src/screens/main/Send.tsx:278-282`; `src/components/Identicon.tsx:38-49`.

Inbox and activity rows are titled `memo ?? counterparty` — up to 2048 bytes of
sender-chosen text where a name is expected, with no control, bidirectional or
zero-width filtering. Memos and vouch statements go verbatim into notification
bodies with no lock-screen visibility set. The Send review shows a truncated
address (9 + 5 characters) and an identicon carrying about 18 bits; the full
recipient is never displayed before "Sign & propose".

### RRN-M-028 · Low — The background credential survives unpairing

**Where:** `src/network/backgroundCredential.ts:83-92`; `src/screens/main/PairedStations.tsx:65-77`;
`src/network/stationNonce.ts:104-120`; `src/network/stationCursor.ts:88-102`.

The code and the August report (RRN-M-007) say the credential is cleared when
the last station is unpaired. `unpair()` only removes the station; the only
caller of `clearBackgroundCredential` is the notification-settings toggle. The
full-power signing blob and its secret stay in the keychain. `clearNonce` and
`clearCursor` ("called when unpairing") have no non-test callers.

### RRN-M-029 · Low — CI and release

- `yarn npm audit` reports 54 advisories at the audited commit, almost all in
  build tooling; three packages ship in the app bundle (`nanoid` via
  `@react-navigation/native`, `decode-uri-component` via `query-string`,
  `text-encoding` via `react-native-qrcode-svg`), none on an attacker-reachable
  path today. CI has no advisory step, so nothing would surface a new one.
- The workflow token is write-scoped by repository default and the workflows
  declare no `permissions`; third-party actions are pinned by tag; `main` has no
  branch protection; Dependabot and secret scanning are off.
- A release build silently falls back to the stock debug keystore when the
  release properties are absent (`android/app/build.gradle:121`). Failing the
  build would be safer than a note in `SIDELOAD.md`.
- Pilot users receive `app-release.apk` and are told to install it. Nothing they
  are given — a certificate fingerprint, a checksum — lets them check it is the
  maintainer's build.
- RRN-M-006 (August) is still open: `enableProguardInReleaseBuilds = false`.
- The `.station-version` pin (`0c4e42f`) correctly builds the FFI from the
  station commit that introduced the fingerprint.

### RRN-M-030 · Info — Smaller observations and stale copy

- The phone POSTs to the stored station host on every network it joins: while
  unlocked every ≤30 s, and every ~15 min in the background. On iOS the host is
  the station's `.local.` name, queried by mDNS on foreign LANs. ADR-0008
  accepts metadata exposure to the community's LAN, not to every network.
- `Home.tsx:164` says "New payments will send when you reconnect." Nothing is
  queued; the app is online-only for signing. `ledger/outbox.ts` is an in-memory
  display list with a misleading name and header; `Send.tsx:10-11` describes an
  outbox hand-off that does not exist.
- `TransactionDetail.tsx:287-303` renders "✓ Verified" for sender and receiver
  from the state string alone; no signature is checked on the device, and "View
  on station log" is a placeholder.
- `pairedStation.ts:9-12` says the transport re-discovers a paired station by
  address when the host stops answering; no such code exists.
- The threat model says uninstall wipes keychain entries on both platforms; iOS
  keychain items normally survive uninstall. It also describes biometric unlock
  as the default; the app requires the passphrase on every foreground, with
  biometrics as an optional extra gate.
- `Info.plist` has no `NSFaceIDUsageDescription` although the keychain uses a
  biometry ACL; iOS is not the pilot platform and the runtime effect was not
  verified.
- Crash reports are copied to the clipboard without the "may contain
  identifiers" line August suggested.

## What was checked and found sound

- No secret crosses the FFI: no seed accessor is exposed; `RecoverySession`
  returns only a `WalletContents` handle; `respond_to_recovery` reads the secret
  inside Rust.
- One persistence backend (the secure store); no AsyncStorage, MMKV or file
  writes under `src/`; `factoryReset` deletes every `SecureStoreKeys` entry.
- Passphrases and export text travel in React context, never in navigation
  params; no deep links, no `linking` config, the only `Linking.openURL` is a
  constant repository URL; the manifest declares one activity and
  `allowBackup="false"`; iOS keychain items are `WHEN_UNLOCKED_THIS_DEVICE_ONLY`
  and ATS allows local networking only.
- `RecoverFromExport` stores nothing until the final seal and clears its inputs;
  `RecoverFromCircle` validates addresses through Rust, refuses a wrong-ceremony
  response, and can never return a wrong key (the Rust address check);
  `HelpRecover` seals only to the request's key and rejects an address mismatch.
- Every reply is opened with the wallet key, verified against the paired
  station's key over the exact bytes, version-checked and nonce-matched, with a
  typed error and no fallback on failure; a 401 triggers no automatic re-pair.
- The request envelope is canonical dCBOR, signed then sealed, with the nonce
  reserved under a lock and persisted before send.
- The pairing reply is verified over `TAG ‖ station_pk ‖ token` with a fresh
  32-byte token per request; the stored address is re-derived from the verified
  key; nothing is persisted until "Codes match — pair"; the mDNS `address`
  claim is never used for trust; discovery only lists, never auto-connects.
- Native discovery: the iOS TXT parser uses bounded `dns_sd` accessors; the
  Android resolve queue is serialized; the port is range-checked; no crash path
  from attacker-controlled TXT or instance names was found by reading.
- Record builders carry distinct `kind`s and go through the Rust encoder;
  `int()` rejects non-integers; `parseCommons` is integer-only with at most two
  decimals and a safe-integer bound; Send signs exactly the receiver, amount and
  memo shown on the review step.
- Background sync is off by default, signs only the transport envelope, advances
  its cursor only on a verified reply, and never throws; notification taps carry
  no payload.
- `CommonMark.tsx` is the ₡ glyph, not a Markdown renderer: listing, proposal,
  charter and memo text render as plain `<Text>` with no links, images or HTML.
- The crash ring is capped at twenty entries and no error path found puts a
  passphrase or key in a message.
- `DevCryptoSmoke` is imported nowhere and unreachable in any build.

## Documentation that the code does not back

- `SECURITY.md:48-49` and the station threat model's mobile sections: Android
  entries are "device-unlock accessible" and the gate is "biometric-gated"
  (RRN-M-021); keychain wipe on uninstall; biometric-first unlock; iOS
  `BIOMETRY_ANY` applied unconditionally (it is opt-in).
- `backgroundCredential.ts:83-86`, `constants.ts:68-70`, RRN-M-007: cleared on
  last unpair (RRN-M-028).
- `SignCharter.tsx:13-15`: "can never sign a body that differs from the one on
  offer" (RRN-M-014).
- `RecoveryIntro.tsx:66-68`: "old pieces stop working" (RRN-M-020).
- `GenerateWallet.tsx:8`: the passphrase is wiped once sealing succeeds — the

  (RRN-M-013).
- `Home.tsx:164`, `ledger/outbox.ts`, `Send.tsx:10-11`: an outbox that does not

  guidance warns against.
